A letter, from me to you

الحب ليس أن تجد نفسك في شخص آخر، بل أن تكتشف في نفسك ما لم تكن تعرفه قبل أن تحب.

Leila,

When I was eighteen, I found myself reading a book about Sufism and the philosophy of love. I remember being fascinated by the idea that love is not simply a feeling, but a journey. A journey through seven different stages, each revealing something about the beloved, but perhaps even more about ourselves.

The first is Hubb (الحب), when someone enters your life and awakens something within you. A curiosity, an affection, a desire to know them beyond what the eyes can see.

Then comes Uns (الأُنس), the feeling of familiarity and comfort. When someone's presence begins to feel like home, when silence is no longer awkward, and when you stop counting the hours you spend together.

The third is Ishq (العشق), when affection grows into something deeper. When someone becomes special to you in ways you cannot entirely explain.

Then comes Shawq (الشوق), the longing for someone even after they have just left.

And Walah (الوله), when love begins to escape the boundaries of reason, leaving you bewildered by the depth of your own feelings.

The sixth is Fana (الفناء), the dissolution of the ego, when love teaches you to care without possessing, to give without demanding.

And finally, Baqa (البقاء), an enduring love that no longer depends on intensity, but on acceptance, patience, and the freedom to love without control.

At eighteen, I thought I was simply reading about love. I never imagined that one day I would find myself trying to understand my own feelings through those same words.

And then came Tala.

I don't know exactly when I moved beyond the first stage. Perhaps it happened somewhere between our conversations, our laughter, the little things we shared, and the moments when neither of us felt the need to be anyone other than ourselves.

But I know something changed.

We spent twelve hours together, Leila. Twelve hours.

And strangely, I never felt the time passing. Not once did I find myself wondering what we should do next or when the day would end. I only felt warmth, comfort, and a kind of happiness that I hadn't expected.

It was only afterward that I realized how much those hours meant to me.

And perhaps that's when I understood Uns. Perhaps I had already begun moving beyond it, toward Ishq.

It's not easy for me to admit that I might be 3asheq (عاشق). It's a word I've always considered too profound to use carelessly. And even now, I hesitate, not because I'm afraid of what I feel, but because I respect what the word means.

Yet there's something else I remember from reading about Sufism.

Love is not merely about finding someone who makes you happy. It is also about discovering who you become when you love.

Rumi and other Sufi thinkers understood love as something transformative. Something that teaches patience, humility, vulnerability, and the ability to set aside one's ego.

And perhaps this is what I find most beautiful about what I'm experiencing.

I'm learning to enjoy something without rushing to define it. To care without expecting anything in return. To appreciate someone's presence without needing to claim it. To let something beautiful grow at its own pace.

I don't know where this journey will take me, or which stage I might find myself in tomorrow.

But I know that these feelings have already taught me something.

The deepest love is not necessarily the most intense. Sometimes, it is the one that quietly changes the way you see the world, yourself, and the person standing in front of you.

And perhaps that's what made me grow even more attached to her. Not simply because of how happy she makes me feel when we're together, but because of how her presence has begun to change the way I think, the way I see myself, and even the way I imagine my future.

I find myself wanting to become a better version of myself, not because she has asked me to change, but because knowing her has given me another reason to grow. The things I work toward, the dreams I carry, and the life I hope to build have somehow begun to feel different. For the first time, I catch myself imagining a future that might have room for both of us.

And I don't know whether that future will ever become ours. Perhaps it is too early to know. But what I find beautiful is that even the possibility of it has already changed something within me.

Maybe that's what Ishq really means to me. Not losing myself in someone else, but discovering parts of myself I never knew existed because of her.

And perhaps that's the most beautiful thing about loving her: she hasn't taken me away from who I am. She's made me want to discover who I could become.

And maybe that's why those twelve hours felt like nothing at all.

Because for once, I wasn't thinking about time.

I was simply happy to be there.
